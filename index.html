Seer Warns.

You were warned.

Twenty standalone novels, one small American town. In each one, a stranger with still hands sits down across from an ordinary person, names the exact cost of the choice they are about to make, and leaves. Every protagonist believes the warning, because the stranger knows the one thing only they could know. Every protagonist makes the choice anyway.

The warning.

Every Seer Warns novel runs the same engine. It is not a ghost story. It is the most ordinary thing in the world, told the moment before it happens.

1

The Seer sits down.

A stranger, in a diner booth, on a bus, at the end of a dock, with hands so still they look finished. The world goes a half-beat quiet. No one else notices. Then the stranger speaks, and names no date and no number.

2

One impossible detail.

The stranger names a single private thing only the protagonist could know, and leaves one object behind. That is the part that cannot be argued away. The future was never written in a secret place; it was written on a page anyone could read.

3

The choice comes anyway.

Loss A or Loss B. To stop is to break the promise that made them who they are, so they don’t. Each book is a warned choice, taken. More on the pattern →

The Honest Woman

Nineteen years in the basement office of Harmon Town Hall, and twenty years of falsified water reports in a drawer the town clerk finally opens. Six hundred jobs hang on the plant her own family founded. The woman at the license bureau names exactly what filing the truth will cost her. She files it anyway.

The Quiet Man

Haskell Hardware, the counter Boyd’s father built in 1971, and the last room where his son Tyler existed, the pencil marks on the door frame, age 5 to 17. When the chain opens up the highway, the old man in Boyd’s booth at the Lamplighter reads him the load path of his own ruin. He opens the store anyway.

The Loyal Man

A county property assessor who has never, in forty-four years, found the word that comes before no to his brother. One date moved in the database, eleven seconds of keyboard, and the system logs everything. A stranger on the Route 22 bus names the whole of the next five months. He believes every word, and does nothing.

One town holds them all.

Every book happens in Harmon, on the same streets, among the same people, in the same handful of rooms. Read three and you begin to recognize the diner booth, the hospital floor, the name on the hardware-store sign. The town is the through-line; the people are the variables.

Standalone, and cumulative.

Closer in shape to Cheever’s Shady Hill or Munro’s Jubilee than to a serialized arc. Begin anywhere. You will meet these people again, in someone else’s book.

·

Reads standalone

No prior reading, no recurring hero to track. Pick any one cold and it stands on its own.

·

The cast accumulates

A background face in one book is the protagonist of another. The connections reward the patient reader without ever being required.
