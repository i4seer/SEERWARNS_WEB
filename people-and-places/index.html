Residents & families

People

One protagonist per book, and the families and neighbors who orbit each one. The cast accumulates as the series grows, the ER nurse you meet in book one is still on shift when the burning-out doctor comes through years later; the careful county assessor who appears at a clerk’s basement window has his own book waiting. Everyone here lives in the same town. Below: the twenty whose turn it is to take the booth, then the recurring faces who carry the town between them.

The twenty.

Each book follows one ordinary person through the year the stranger came for them. They are listed here in the series’ recommended reading order; every story stands alone.

  • David Marsh · history teacher, Harmon High, The Good Father
  • Ruth Calloway · town clerk, The Honest Woman
  • Boyd Haskell · hardware-store owner, The Quiet Man
  • Dennis Reilly · county property assessor, The Loyal Man
  • Cat Brennan · ER physician, St. Clare’s, The Tired Mother
  • Diana Torres · corporate attorney, The Lawyer Daughter
  • Elena Voss · retired librarian, widow, The Tending Woman
  • Maggie Caldwell · contractor, Caldwell Construction, The Loyal Daughter
  • Nora Finch · marina manager, The Saved Woman
  • Jack Callahan · the returning exile, The Accused Man
  • Neil Mercer · neurosurgeon, St. Clare’s, The Steady Hand
  • Marcus Cole · county road maintenance, The Ambitious Son
  • Anne Loring · architect, Loring & Finch, The Design Partner
  • Frank Morrow · the returning son, The Distant Man
  • David Rourke · pastor, First Baptist, The Silent Pastor
  • James Ota · graphic designer, The Distant Husband

The Marsh family.

From The Good Father. The first family the series follows, and the family it uses to set its terms. Four people at a breakfast table; the book counts the plates as they disappear.

  • David Marsh · history teacher, Harmon High, Room 217
  • Sarah Marsh · ER nurse, St. Clare’s, the recurring hospital face
  • Emma Marsh · daughter, fourteen, the one who stops speaking
  • Cooper Marsh · son, eight, travel-league ball, the one who still believes
  • Helen Marsh · David’s mother, night office-cleaner, Room 14B at Lakeview

Around the Marshes.

The colleagues, the friend, and the handicapper who orbit The Good Father.

  • Rick Soto · math teacher at Harmon High, the friend who made it sound like math
  • Karen Silvestri · principal of Harmon High, who held the door longer than the form allowed
  • Hank · the OTB handicapper who taught David to read a racing form

The Calloway family.

From The Honest Woman. The clerk who keeps the record, and the family the plant fed for three generations, the family whose name is on the plant.

  • Ruth Calloway · town clerk, nineteen years in the basement office
  • Diane Calloway-Park · Ruth’s sister, hairdresser on Main Street
  • Tom Park · Diane’s husband, twenty-two years on the plant kill floor
  • Chester · Ruth’s golden retriever, asleep under the Town Hall desk

The Haskells.

From The Quiet Man. A store, a marriage, and a pencil mark on a door frame.

  • Boyd Haskell · owner, Haskell Hardware, thirty-one years behind the counter
  • Jean Haskell · his wife, retired elementary-school secretary
  • Tyler Haskell · their son, killed in Afghanistan in 2014 at nineteen; the door frame holds his heights, age 5 to 17

The Reilly family.

From The Loyal Man, and a Main Street fixture across the whole series. Three generations behind one register and three brothers who took different roads out of it: the quiet one who kept the store, the generous one who keeps the favors, and the careful one who keeps the honest books.

The grandparents · founders

  • Patrick Reilly · founded Reilly’s Sporting Goods in 1957 (1909–2009)
  • Maeve O’Brien Reilly · Patrick’s wife, the family’s grandmother (d. 2028)

The father

  • Bill Reilly · Patrick’s son, ran the store after him until his death in 2005

The three brothers · and their households

  • Brian Reilly · the eldest, fifty, keeps Reilly’s Sporting Goods, the quiet one · wife Patrice
  • Kevin Reilly · the middle brother, forty-eight, Reilly Property Group — laundromats and storage — the private note against Dennis’s house, the Lexus
  • Lorraine Reilly · Kevin’s wife, third-grade teacher at Harmon Elementary
  • Tyler Reilly · Kevin & Lorraine’s son, twelve
  • Megan Reilly · Kevin & Lorraine’s daughter, eleven
  • Dennis Reilly · the youngest, forty-four, senior county property assessor, sixteen years at the same desk
  • Pam Reilly · Dennis’s wife, forty-three, fourth-grade teacher at Harmon Elementary
  • Blake Reilly · Dennis & Pam’s son, twelve, the one who gets the ham-and-swiss sandwich
  • Katie Reilly · Dennis & Pam’s daughter, nine, draws horses, keeps the volume at six

Extended · and Pam’s friend

  • Walter Reilly · the brothers’ older cousin, longtime Reilly Hardware owner out in Cooperville
  • Maureen · Kevin’s first wife, now Pam’s friend of twenty-two years and a third-grade teacher — the one who does not stop coming around

Around the family, in The Loyal Man

  • Frank Delgado · Dennis’s supervisor, a former Marine, who says it in three sentences
  • Maria Gutierrez · the county forensic accountant who finds the edit in eleven minutes (also The Design Partner)

The Brennan household.

From The Tired Mother. A doctor who can read a whole town, and the patient she keeps missing in her own house.

  • Cat Brennan · ER physician, St. Clare’s
  • Miles Brennan · her son, too quiet, too stable
  • Ray Brennan · her late husband, whose deathbed standing order she can’t discharge
  • Gloria · Ray’s mother, in slow decline out in Marl

The Torres family.

From The Lawyer Daughter. The North End family that built much of the North End, and the drawer the father left locked.

  • Diana Torres · corporate attorney, the good daughter
  • Ernesto Torres · her late father, founder of Torres Construction
  • Rosa Torres · his widow, still in the brick house on Riverside Avenue

Recurring faces of the town.

Not protagonists, but the connective tissue, the people who carry the town between the books and turn up, named and the same, across many of them.

  • Donna · the Lamplighter waitress since 1979, who pours the coffee without asking and keeps the wall of clippings
  • Margie Pearson · owner-cook of the Lamplighter since 1995, who makes the pie from scratch and keeps the quiet watch on the rooms
  • Hannah Calloway · the Sentinel’s town-hall reporter, Ruth’s niece, who writes what goes on the wall
  • Dr. Patricia Alvarez · palliative-care attending at St. Clare’s since 1998, the medical witness across the hospital books
  • Maria Gutierrez · the county forensic accountant who finds what everyone else walks past

The figure who is in every book.

Not a person on this list, and yet the one who appears in all twenty. The Seer, the calm stranger with the still hands and the brown pencil, sits down across from each protagonist exactly once, and is glimpsed in the background of every other story: a man in the corner booth of the Lamplighter, a passenger on the Route 22 bus, a figure at the end of the marina dock. The rooms of Harmon

Places

A location named once is the same location every time. The third booth at the diner is the same booth in book one and book nine; the plant in the Flats is the same plant whose closing one book mourns and another exposes. Below: every place in Harmon, by kind, the establishments on Main Street, the civic buildings, the districts, the homes, the waterfront, and the roads characters take to leave. Explore them all on the interactive map.

Main Street & downtown.

Three blocks of the old commercial core, half-vacant now. The Lamplighter, Roasters, and Haskell Hardware run east in that order.

  • The Lamplighter · the diner; the Seer’s recurring booth, pouring since 1977
  • Roasters · the coffee shop between the diner and the hardware store; a Seer venue in later books
  • Haskell Hardware · built 1962, the counter 1971; the pencil marks on the door frame
  • The Harmon Sentinel · the county weekly; the record the town keeps of itself, printed every Thursday
  • Reilly’s Sporting Goods · founded 1957; the bell, the oak counter, the change put in your hand
  • First Federal · Harmon’s bank; the HELOC, the foreclosure paperwork
  • The barbershop, the florist, the dry cleaner, the Varsity Theater · the south-side row

Civic & institutional.

  • Harmon County Courthouse · Courthouse Square; the courthouse steps recur across the series
  • Harmon Town Hall · Ruth Calloway’s basement clerk’s office
  • St. Clare’s Hospital · Cass & Church; the ER, the ICU, the surgical floor, four books work here
  • Harmon High School · David Marsh’s Room 217; Warren Gibbs’s English room below
  • Harmon Elementary · far East Side; Cooper Marsh, Pam Reilly’s classroom
  • First Baptist Church · David Rourke’s pulpit, on Bishop Hill
  • St. Anne’s Catholic Church · the Torres family parish, Fourth Street, North End

Eldercare, out Route 9.

The far end of the rural road, where the town sends the people it cannot keep.

  • Lakeview Memory Care · Helen Marsh’s Room 14B; the gap that opens The Good Father
  • Pine Ridge Senior Care · the cheaper place, since 1978; the one David refuses

The plant & the Flats.

  • Calloway–Harmon plant · the pork plant, the town’s engine and its wound; closed December 2024
  • The grain elevator · on the rail spur; the Harmon County Fairgrounds beside it

The big-box bypass & the West Edge.

  • Harmon Supercenter · the first big-box store on the bypass
  • ValueMart · the middle store; the grocery run as town decline
  • HomePlus · the chain whose grand-opening circular ends Haskell Hardware
  • The strip mall & the OTB · the West Edge, off Route 9; The Good Father’s gambling geography

The waterfront.

  • Lake Harmon · the drinking water, the public beach, the lake the children swam in
  • Harmon Harbor Marina · Nora Finch’s marina; the only outdoor Seer encounter, at the end of the dock
  • The Harmon (lake) tunnel · Route 11 north under the lake toward Bridgeport

The districts.

  • Downtown · Main Street, the historic core
  • The North End · the oldest neighborhood, the numbered grid, St. Anne’s
  • The East Side · the hospital, the high school, the tree-streets, Bishop Hill, Pinewood
  • The waterfront · north of Water Street; marina, park, beach
  • The Flats · the plant, the river, the grain elevator, the fairgrounds

Homes & the roads out.

  • The Marsh house · 1847 Third Street, North End (The Good Father)
  • The Torres house · Riverside Avenue, North End (The Lawyer Daughter)
  • Elena Voss’s bungalow · Maple Drive, Pinewood (The Tending Woman)
  • Lakeview Memory Care & Pine Ridge Senior Care · out Route 9 west, beyond Marl
  • Route 9 (Marl ↔ Hartwick), Route 11 (the capital ↔ Bridgeport), Route 6 (the eastern back road)
